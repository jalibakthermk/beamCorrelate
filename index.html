Conventions. H is positive to the right (R), V positive upward (U), seen from behind the lamp. ΔH/ΔV is the shift applied to the simulation so it lines up with the measurement. Type A/B IES files and CSV grids are read as H-V angles directly. Type C (IES/LDT) is converted assuming the optical axis at C0/γ90 with C90 to the right; tick "Mirror left/right" if a beam comes out flipped.

CSV. Matrix layout with H angles across the first row and V angles down the first column (tick "CSV rows are H" if transposed), or three columns H, V, cd. Comma, semicolon or tab separated.

Ratio. Measured / simulated, on a log₂ colour scale so ×2 and ×0.5 look equally far from ×1. RMS deviation and the tolerance band are computed on unmasked cells only.

Cut-off. Vertical scans in 0.05° steps on log-interpolated data. Sharpness G = log₁₀ I(β) − log₁₀ I(β+0.1°), taken at its maximum. The cut-off position β is the centre of the gradient peak (inflection of log I). Linearity is the spread of β across the scan positions. The elbow is where a straight-line fit of the rising shoulder meets the horizontal part, and the shoulder angle comes from that fit. G is only as good as the data resolution near the cut-off.
